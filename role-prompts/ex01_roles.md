    > Geologist
    A rock is a naturally occurring solid aggregate of one or more minerals (or mineraloids), often including organic material or glass.
    In geological terms, rocks are the fundamental building blocks of Earth’s crust (and the crusts of other rocky planets and moons). They form through a variety of natural processes and are classified primarily by how they form:
    
    =The three major rock types

    -Igneous rocks
    Formed by the cooling and solidification of molten rock (magma below the surface or lava at the surface).
    Intrusive (plutonic) examples: granite, gabbro
    Extrusive (volcanic) examples: basalt, rhyolite, obsidian

    -Sedimentary rocks
    Formed by the accumulation, compaction, and cementation of sediments (or by chemical precipitation).
    Clastic examples: sandstone, shale, conglomerate
    Chemical/biochemical examples: limestone, rock salt, coal

    -Metamorphic rocks
    Formed when pre-existing rocks are transformed by heat, pressure, and/or chemically active fluids without melting.
    Examples: marble (from limestone), slate (from shale), gneiss, schist, quartzite


    =Key distinctions

    A mineral is a naturally occurring, inorganic solid with a definite chemical composition and an ordered atomic structure (e.g., quartz, feldspar, calcite).
    A rock is usually a mixture of minerals. Some rocks (e.g., pure quartzite or rock salt) can be essentially monomineralic, and a few (obsidian, coal) are non-crystalline or organic in origin.

    Rocks are continually recycled through the rock cycle — melting, weathering, erosion, deposition, burial, and metamorphism — driven by plate tectonics, climate, and gravity.
    In short: if you can pick it up and it is a coherent, naturally formed piece of Earth’s solid material (not a single pure mineral crystal or an artificial object), you’re looking at a rock.


    > Contemporary Poet
    A rock
    is the earth’s quiet fist,
    clenched for a billion years
    and still not ready to let go.
    It does not speak.
    It simply is—
    granite patience,
    basalt indifference,
    the original hard truth
    before we invented soft ones.
    You can hold it
    and feel the weight of continents
    that once drifted like gossip.
    You can throw it
    and for a second it flies
    like something that almost remembered
    it used to be lava,
    or star.
    Mostly it just sits.
    In rivers it learns patience.
    In deserts it learns heat.
    Under cities it learns
    how little we notice
    what holds us up.
    A rock is the part of the world
    that refused to become
    anything more convenient.
