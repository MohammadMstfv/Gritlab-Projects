    PROFESSIONALLY:
    As a chef, the key to a perfect sunny-side up egg is low, gentle heat and leaving the yolk untouched. You want a fully set white, a glossy runny yolk, and no crispy brown edges.

    Yields: 1–2 eggs
    Equipment: nonstick or carbon-steel skillet, lid, spatula, small bowl

    Ingredients

    2 large eggs, as fresh as possible
    1 tbsp butter, or 1 tsp neutral oil
    Kosher salt
    Freshly ground black pepper
    Optional: chives, toast, hot sauce
    Chef’s Method

    Prep the eggs
    Crack each egg into a small ramekin or bowl. This prevents broken yolks and shell bits.
    Heat the pan correctly
    Place an 8-inch nonstick skillet over medium-low heat. Add butter. It should melt, foam, and smell nutty—but not brown or smoke. If using oil, it should shimmer, not ripple.
    Add the eggs gently
    Slide the eggs from the ramekin into the pan, spacing them apart. Do not crowd the pan. If you want perfect circles, use an egg ring.
    Cook low and slow
    Let them sit 2–3 minutes without touching. The whites should turn opaque and set from the outside in. If the edges start bubbling aggressively or browning, lower the heat immediately.
    Set the top of the white
    You have two professional options:

    Baste: Tilt the pan slightly and spoon hot butter/oil over the whites, avoiding the yolk. This gives a silky top.
    Steam: Add 1 tsp water to the pan, cover with a lid, and cook 30–60 seconds until the white is fully set but the yolk is still runny.
    Season and serve
    Season the whites with salt and pepper—not the yolk, or it will speckle. Add chives if using. Slide onto toast or a plate. Serve immediately.
    Chef’s Notes

    Fresh eggs hold their shape best. Older eggs spread thinner.
    Never flip a sunny-side up egg. That makes it over-easy.
    If the white is still runny near the yolk, cover and steam a few more seconds.
    For a richer flavor, finish with a tiny knob of cold butter off the heat.
    If you’re concerned about food safety, use pasteurized eggs or cook until the yolk is firm.
    Perfect sunny-side up: white set, yolk liquid gold, no brown crust.
